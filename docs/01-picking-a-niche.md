# Chapter 1 — Picking a Niche That Survives Contact With Reality

"Pick a niche" is the least-caressed advice on the internet. Here is the
caressed version, tuned for a *hobby* blog — something you do for joy,
not a growth-hacking machine.

## 1.1 The two-question test

Answer these honestly, in writing:

1. **Would I still do this if nobody read it?**
   A hobby blog gets most of its value from the *doing*. If the process
   itself is enjoyable — photographing your sourdough, logging your chess
   games, sketching your minis — readers become a bonus, not a
   prerequisite.
2. **Can I name five things I'd write about *today*?**
   Not someday, today. If you can list five posts before you start, you
   have a niche. If you can't, you have a mood.

If you pass both, you have a niche. It does not need to be narrower than
"pottery" or narrower than "1970s Japanese film cameras." Those are both
fine.

## 1.2 The slice, not the field

You do not need to be the world expert. You need to be the world's
*friendliest window* into your corner of it. The winning angle is almost
always documentation, not expertise:

| Field      | Too broad            | A workable slice                                  |
|------------|----------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| Chess      | "Chess"              | "What rated ~800 games taught me each week"        |
| Gardening  | "Gardening"          | "A balcony garden in a cold climate, one season"   |
| Gaming     | "Games I like"       | "A newer player's first year in FFXIV"             |
| Cooking    | "Recipes"            | "One-pan dinners I can actually make after work"   |

A slice works because it forces *narrative* (there is a you, with
constraints, moving forward) instead of *reference material* (you are
competing with ten thousand SEO-dusted listicles).

## 1.3 Names and domains — later, or never

Resist the temptation to buy a domain before you have written three
posts. Use the free one first:

```
https://yourname.github.io/
```

That URL does not expire in a year, costs nothing, and survives until
you, personally, decide to move. Chapter 8 covers when a custom domain
is actually worth money.

## 1.4 The no-burnout starter plan

- Write **three** posts *before* you publish anything. This becomes your
  launch buffer and your proof the niche is real.
- Cap your early ambition: one post per week is plenty. Chapter 7
  explains why weekly beats daily for a hobby.
- Do not build social accounts, mailing lists, or a logo yet. Ship one
  post per week for a month first.

## 1.5 A checklist you can paste everywhere

- [ ] I named five post ideas today.
- [ ] I would write even with zero readers.
- [ ] I chose a slice with a "me" in it.
- [ ] I have not paid anyone anything.
- [ ] I opened a GitHub repository named after my blog.

If all five boxes are checked, move to Chapter 2.
