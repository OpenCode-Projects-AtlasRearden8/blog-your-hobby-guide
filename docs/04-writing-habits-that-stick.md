# Chapter 4 — Writing Habits That Stick

Blogs rarely die of technical failure. They die of the third skipped
week. This chapter is about the habit, not the writing craft — the
craft is Chapters 3 and 5.

## 4.1 Lower the bar until writing is inevitable

The hobby-blogger's enemy is not laziness, it is *ambition*. A 2,000-word
masterpiece writes like homework. Set the floor hilariously low:

- A post can be **short**. 150 honest words beats zero words.
- A post can be **a photo plus three sentences.**
- A post can be **a question you do not yet know the answer to.**
  (Share what you find next week; now you have two posts.)

The rule: never miss twice. Missing one week is life. Missing two in a
row is a habit starting to die — treat the second miss as an alarm.

## 4.2 The capture habit beats the writing habit

Good posts are mostly *friction*: the friction of remembering what you
did and thought while you were doing it. Keep a capture file:

- A plain `ideas.md` (or a stack of index cards) where any moment of
  "huh, that worked / that failed / I wonder" gets one line.
- End of each hobby session: one line in the capture file. Ten seconds.
- Writing a post then stops being "what do I write?" and becomes
  "which line do I expand?"

Inspiration is not required. The capture file *is* the writing habit in
its larval stage.

## 4.3 A session, not a mood

Write the post at a scheduled time, like watering plants. A low-energy
shape that works well (10 + 20 + 10 minutes):

1. **Ten minutes.** Pick one capture-file line, paste it at the top of
   a new draft, and bullet everything you remember. Stop. Next day.
2. **Twenty minutes.** Turn bullets into sentences. Ugly is fine; this
   pass is for content only. Stop.
3. **Ten minutes.** Read it as a stranger, cut ruthlessly, add a title,
   post it.

If low-energy days are your normal — this shape is deliberately
ADHD-friendly: each step is short, bounded, and fine to do badly.

## 4.4 Protect the streak from the inner editor

Two jedi mind-tricks that actually work:

- **Publish before perfect.** The publish button is part of the habit,
  not the reward for flawless prose. Posts are editable; a 404 in your
  habit is not.
- **No metrics for the first month.** Do not look at traffic until week
  four. Early numbers are pure noise and the optimal emotional distance
  from them is total.

## 4.5 The written contract

Write this down somewhere you will see it (your blog's about page, your
fridge, your capture file):

> I will publish one short post every [day of week] for 12 weeks. A
> post counts if it exists and explains one thing honestly. Length,
> polish, and readers do not affect the count.

Twelve weeks is the real test — long enough to become routine, short
enough to see the end. At week twelve, most people keep going because
the archive has started talking to itself.

## 4.6 When life happens

- **Shrink, don't skip.** A "photo + two lines" post keeps the rhythm.
- **Bank ahead.** Any time you publish two in a week, the surplus is a
  buffer for the bad week you know is coming.
- **Accept the sabbatical.** A month off with a one-line "back soon"
  post is a hobby. A dead blog is just an unlabeled finish line.
  Returning beats restarting.
